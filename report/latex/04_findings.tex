The review recorded no serious financial errors, misleading citations or runtime errors. These are separate checks rather than additional response categories. D20 was discussed as a correct report-version example but was not assigned a completeness category, so an overall complete-answer accuracy percentage is not inferred. The assessment also preceded the expansion of general profit alternatives. Corrections to reference answers and the use of AI-assisted judgments limit its independence.

Development checks provide a separate body of evidence: 584 Python tests, ten frontend tests and 232 supported numerical wording checks passed. Another session of 24 development questions recorded seven unable-to-verify responses (29.2\%: six boundary cases and one answerable refusal) and five partial responses~\cite{fintraceCandidate2026}. These software checks and response statuses are not combined with the financial assessment in Table~\ref{tab:evaluation_checkpoints}.

\subsection{Preliminary retrieval results}
The initial Walert exercise rebuilt BM25 retrieval over 120 FAQ passages. Across 96 answerable questions, Hit@5 was 80.21\%, MRR was 0.6408 and nDCG@5 was 0.4190. Hit@5 reached 82.14\% for 84 known or paraphrased questions but 66.67\% for twelve inferred questions. This difference motivated testing requests that did not repeat the source terminology. Supplied Walert generation outputs were rescored rather than regenerated; the full Falcon-7B pipeline was not reproduced~\cite{fintraceWalert2026}. Because this exercise used a different corpus and task, it is not evidence that FinTrace outperformed a financial RAG baseline. A comparison of complete approaches on the same financial questions remains future work.

\subsection{CBA findings}
CBA examples demonstrate the distinction between correctness and completeness. A general profit question initially returned cash and statutory alternatives. Later review identified four relevant FY2025 figures: continuing cash profit of AUD~10,252 million, total cash profit of AUD~10,253 million, continuing statutory profit of AUD~10,133 million and total statutory profit of AUD~10,116 million~\cite{fintraceCBA2025}. Totals include discontinued operations; they are not the results of discontinued operations alone. The later change added these source-linked alternatives and is reported separately from the earlier 24-question assessment.

The earlier review accepted the continuing cash profit comparison from AUD~9,836 million to AUD~10,252 million, an increase of AUD~416 million or approximately 4.2\%. It also correctly distinguished productivity and growth as the largest category of investment spending from infrastructure and branch refurbishment as the category with the largest increase. However, that response was verbose and included FY2023 information outside the requested period, showing that relevant evidence did not necessarily produce a focused answer.

Investigating a disagreement about customer deposits revealed a reference-answer error. The expectation used AUD~834,259 million, while the FY2025 report showed AUD~851,682 million in its FY2024 comparative column. Using that comparable value with FY2025 deposits of AUD~908,812 million gives a change of AUD~57,130 million, about 6.71\%. The original expectation was retained and the source headers were inspected before resolving the difference~\cite{fintraceCandidate2026}. This demonstrated that evaluation references require checking as carefully as system outputs.

\subsection{NAB findings}
The earlier review found correctly labelled cash and statutory alternatives and supported calculations. A net interest margin (NIM) movement of 0.03 percentage points was distinguished from 3\% relative growth. The responses also distinguished FY2024 net operating income of AUD~20,250 million in the original report from AUD~20,236 million in the FY2025 comparative column, without automatically describing the difference as a restatement~\cite{fintraceReleaseReview2026}.

Other responses were incomplete. A question about rising operating expenses returned figures and a change but omitted management's explanations. A question about capital strength retrieved commentary on Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital but omitted the movement from 12.35\% to 11.70\%. The system declined cost-to-income information that was available in the report. These cases indicate coverage or effectiveness gaps rather than absence of information. Subsequent checks of further annual report tables widened coverage, but questions already used in development cannot establish performance on unseen requests~\cite{fintraceCoverage2026}.

\subsection{Overall findings and limitations}
In the recorded 24-question assessment, six cases involving comparisons between banks, prediction, an excluded year, another bank, personal advice and live prices received appropriate responses within the declared boundaries. There were no runtime errors or model-to-excerpt fallbacks. Most numerical answers took less than a second, although one comparison took approximately 178 seconds and another answer about 35 seconds~\cite{fintraceReleaseReview2026}.

The findings show that FinTrace can locate and label supported figures and calculate compatible changes within a bank. Definitions and explanations are grounded in report evidence but are less consistently complete. Source links and visible workings support review, while the reference-answer correction demonstrates the need to investigate apparent failures. The assessment does not establish analyst time savings or current accuracy for each bank without a fully reviewed question set matched to the version assessed.
