\section{Problem Definition}
\label{sec:Problem Definition}

\subsection{Stakeholder and motivation}
An analyst preparing a briefing on an Australian bank may start with a simple question: ``What was its profit this year?'' Finding a number is only the first step. The analyst still needs to check what it measures, whether it can be compared with last year's result and which report supports it. That information can be spread across results releases, management commentary and accounting notes. A keyword search can find the word ``profit'' without resolving any of those questions.

CBA's FY2025 Profit Announcement is a useful example. Its table gives four profit figures: cash and statutory profit, each for continuing operations and for the total including discontinued operations. Continuing cash profit is AUD~10,252 million, while statutory profit including discontinued operations is AUD~10,116 million. Neither is a typo. They describe different results. If an assistant returns one without explaining its reporting basis and scope, the figure may be copied into a briefing with the wrong meaning even though the number itself appears in the report \cite{fintraceCBA2025}.

Our project addresses this evidence-checking task. The problem is not that the banks' published figures are necessarily wrong, but that an answer can lose the context needed to use them correctly. FinTrace is intended to support an analyst's review before a briefing or sign-off, not replace that judgement. The reporting examples and prototype failures establish the problem within our selected documents; we have not measured its frequency across the industry.

\subsection{Aim and project questions}
FinTrace aims to make reported financial information easier to find and check. Users can ask questions, compare supported results across years, look up financial terms and explore management's explanations, with source pages and calculation workings available for review. Our user story is: \emph{As a financial analyst reviewing bank results, I want to find and compare reported information with traceable evidence, so that I can check its meaning before using it in a briefing.}

The project investigates four questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Can it find the requested information and retain the correct company, year, unit, reporting basis and operational scope?
\item Can it compare compatible results within a bank across two financial years and show the figures and calculation used?
\item Can it explain financial terms and reported changes using relevant source evidence, without presenting an unsupported inference as a disclosed fact?
\item Can it recognise evidence limits and communicate them without inventing an answer or substituting a different measure?
\end{enumerate}
We assess these questions through answer correctness, completeness, citation support, supported calculations and unanswered-question behaviour. Refusing unavailable information can be appropriate; refusing a question that the system should answer is still a gap. Response time is considered separately, since a correct answer also needs to be practical to use. The evaluation sections define the scoring and report what was achieved.

\subsection{Scope and changes since Milestone 1}
Milestone~1 proposed a corporate-report assistant with separate Ask and Verify modes, including claim verdicts. We narrowed this to a single question interface focused on Commonwealth Bank of Australia (CBA) and National Australia Bank (NAB), using six official reports covering FY2024 and FY2025. Keeping one sector and two years made it feasible to understand the terminology, check reference answers and test historical comparisons. The final system uses different answer paths behind the interface rather than separate Ask and Verify products \cite{fintraceMilestone2026,fintraceSources2026}.

We began with 13 core financial-measure families and selected tables. Testing showed that using this catalogue as the boundary for all questions caused unnecessary refusals. We therefore expanded prose search across the full reports and added checked NAB statement and reconciliation tables. General profit and earnings-per-share questions also retain the available, separately labelled figures. This broadened what the assistant could read while keeping calculations tied to checked source values \cite{fintraceCoverage2026,fintraceBroad2026}.

Supported comparisons are within a bank across the available years. Explicit cross-bank comparisons are declined; CBA and NAB also have different financial year-ends. Live market prices, future realised profits, private account information and personalised investment recommendations are outside scope. The deliverable is a local research prototype and an evaluation of its strengths and limits. It demonstrates a way to check answers, but does not establish analyst time savings or readiness for production use.
