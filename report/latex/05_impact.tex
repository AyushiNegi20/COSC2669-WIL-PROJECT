\section{Impact and Significance of Results}
\label{sec:Impact and Significance of Results}

\subsection{Value for financial analysts}
FinTrace supports the preparation of a financial briefing by bringing a reported result, its context and supporting evidence into one interface. An analyst can inspect a figure's reporting period, basis and source before reusing it. This provides a practical alternative to an isolated answer whose meaning must be reconstructed from the report.

The profit example illustrates this value. A user may not know to distinguish cash from statutory profit or continuing from total operations. Displaying the available labelled alternatives makes those distinctions visible. Similarly, a comparison becomes easier to inspect when the underlying values, calculation and report version are shown together.

These functions were demonstrated in the working prototype. The recorded assessment of 24 questions found no serious financial errors or misleading citations, although some answerable questions remained incomplete or were declined~\cite{fintraceReleaseReview2026}. Useful coverage therefore matters alongside the avoidance of incorrect figures: an unnecessary refusal still leaves the analyst with additional work.

\subsection{Expected benefits and responsible use}
The prototype is intended to reduce manual searching and make financial evidence easier to check. These are expected benefits rather than measured improvements in analyst performance. A controlled study comparing manual search and FinTrace on the same briefing tasks could measure completion time, answer quality and verification effort.

Human review remains necessary before financial sign-off. Public reports can omit information, retrieval can miss relevant passages and a quotation may not answer every part of a request. Publication version, entity boundaries and financial definitions must therefore remain visible. Historical results and spending also do not establish future performance or investment suitability.

The configured demo uses local generation rather than a hosted provider. Wider deployment would still require access controls, security review, accessibility testing and validation for multiple users. Those capabilities were not assessed in this project.

\subsection{Future development}
The next priority is feedback from analysts on answer completeness, useful coverage and the presentation of evidence. Improvements should be tested on new questions after fixing the version to be assessed, with earlier failure cases retained as regression tests. Response time should also be measured without competition for hardware resources before operational expectations are set.

Broader use would require dependable installation, monitored report updates and validated accounting relationships. Expansion beyond banking would introduce different terminology and reporting practices, so it requires more than adding PDFs. These steps provide a practical route from the current research prototype towards a tool suitable for its intended users.
